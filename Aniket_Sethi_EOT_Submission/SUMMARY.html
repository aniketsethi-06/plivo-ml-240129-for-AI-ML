End-of-Turn (EOT) Detection: Solution Summary

Project Overview

The objective of this project was to build a model from scratch capable of predicting whether a human conversational turn is over (EOT) or if the user is simply holding the floor, using only audio data preceding the pause. The model strictly adheres to the causality rule, ensuring no future audio is accessed during feature extraction.

Final Score: 295 ms mean response delay (at 3.0% interrupted turns)

Why This Approach Beats the Status Quo

The provided baseline relied entirely on silence duration, resulting in a ~1600 ms delay. Silence is an unreliable indicator because humans frequently utilize long pauses to gather their thoughts ("holds"). By shifting the paradigm from silence-duration to prosodic analysis, the model infers intent.

  • Pitch Drop (Terminal Juncture): The model uses the YIN algorithm to extract F0 and calculates the pitch slope. A trailing pitch strongly indicates an EOT.
  • Energy Decay: By calculating the Mean and Standard Deviation of the RMS energy, the model detects the natural volume decay that occurs at the end of a statement.

Summary of Logs & Notes

As documented in RUNLOG.md, moving from a heuristic silence-based approach to a Random Forest classifier analyzing a 1.5-second trailing prosodic window yielded an immediate 5x improvement in response delay. As noted in NOTES.md, future iterations would benefit from adding zero-crossing rate analysis to detect unvoiced consonants, mitigating edge-case failures where high-energy "thinking noises" mimic active speech.
