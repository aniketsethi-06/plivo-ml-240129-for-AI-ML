# Notes

My model relies heavily on prosodic signals, specifically pitch (F0) dynamics and RMS energy extracted from the final 1.5 seconds of speech preceding a pause. By calculating the pitch slope using the YIN algorithm, the model successfully detects the terminal juncture (a falling pitch) that typically signifies a human has finished speaking. Furthermore, variations in RMS energy help capture the volume trailing off at the end of a conversational turn. The model still occasionally fails on highly enthusiastic or questioning holds where the speaker maintains a high, suspended pitch and energy level despite pausing to think. It can also struggle if the audio segment contains heavy background noise that disrupts the YIN pitch extraction. If I had one more day, I would engineer features to analyze the zero-crossing rate to better capture unvoiced consonants that often end words. I would also experiment with a dynamic trailing window that adjusts based on the overall speech rate of the user, rather than a hardcoded 1.5-second lookback. One caveat: the model also uses the pause's raw position in the turn (time since start) as a feature, which carries real signal but risks encoding this dataset's specific turn-length patterns rather than a cue that transfers cleanly to the hidden test set.
